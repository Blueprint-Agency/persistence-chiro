Chiropractic and massage both use the hands, and people book either one for the same sore back, but they work on different parts of the problem. Chiropractic works on how a restricted joint moves. Massage works on the muscle and soft tissue around it. Neither one is better across the board. ## Chiropractic vs massage: what each one works on

Chiropractic is aimed at joints, massage at soft tissue. A chiropractor looks for spinal segments (the individual joints between vertebrae) that have stopped moving the way they should, and uses a quick, specific adjustment on the ones that are stuck. A massage works through muscle, tendon and fascia (the thin connective wrapping around each muscle) with pressure, kneading and stretching, usually over a wider area.

| | Chiropractic care | Massage |
|---|---|---|
| Mainly works on | How individual spinal joints move | Muscle, tendon and fascia |
| Main tool | A specific adjustment to the joints found to be restricted | Pressure, kneading and stretching through the tissue |
| How the area is chosen | A structured assessment of the spine before anything is adjusted | Where it feels tight, plus anything you point out |
| How it feels | Brief, often with a click, then usually done in a few minutes | Steady pressure for 30 to 90 minutes |
| What you take home | Posture and movement advice, sometimes exercises | Usually nothing beyond feeling looser |

Neither column is the clinical one and the other the soft option. A good massage therapist reads tissue very well, and a chiropractor who skips the assessment is not doing chiropractic properly.

## What research says about chiropractic and massage for back pain

For lower back pain, the honest answer from the research is that both help some people a modest amount, mostly in the short term. A Cochrane review of 25 trials found massage eased pain compared with no care, but mostly in the short term, and the authors rated the evidence low to very low quality. A 2019 review in the BMJ, covering 47 trials of spinal manipulation for chronic lower back pain, found it worked about as well as other recommended options for short-term pain, with a small improvement in day to day function.

The UK's NICE guideline on low back pain puts both in the same group. It lists spinal manipulation and soft tissue work such as massage together as "manual therapy", to consider only alongside exercise, not on their own.

So be wary of any page, including a chiropractor's, telling you one of these gives lasting change and the other only temporary relief. The research does not support that split. What usually matters more is whether the hands on work comes with a plan for what keeps loading the area.

## Joint or muscle? Signs your pain leans towards chiropractic or massage

The pattern of the pain is a better guide than the location. A spot that feels locked, or a movement you simply cannot make, points towards a joint. Tightness spread across a wide area that eases when someone presses on it points towards the muscle.

## Having chiropractic and massage together: order and timing

Having both is common, and it does not mean one of them failed. A joint that has been stiff for months usually has muscle tightened around it, and loosening one does not automatically settle the other.

On order, people do it both ways. Some find that soft tissue work first lets them relax enough for the adjustment to feel easier. Others prefer the adjustment first. No good research settles which order is better, so we decide from what the assessment finds and from what you find more comfortable.

On timing, both can leave you a little tender for a day or so, much like after a hard workout. That is normal and usually settles on its own. Tell each practitioner about the other, especially if you are seeing them at different places. ## When to skip both chiropractic and massage and see a doctor first

Some symptoms need a doctor before anyone works on the area, whether with an adjustment or a massage:

- a calf that is swollen, warm, red or painful for no clear reason, which can be a sign of a blood clot, and firm massage over it is the last thing it needs
- a recent fall or knock where the area is badly bruised, swollen or you cannot put weight on it
- numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or getting worse
- any change in bladder or bowel control alongside back pain
- back or neck pain with a fever, or with weight loss you cannot explain

If any of these sound familiar, see a doctor or go to an emergency department first. Our post on [tit tar and chiropractic](/blog/tit-tar-vs-chiropractic) has a fuller list of what to rule out after a fall or knock.
