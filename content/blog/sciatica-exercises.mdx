Gentle sciatica exercises are one of the most useful things you can do for that pain running down your leg, and staying active usually beats resting in bed. The catch is that sciatica is a symptom, not one condition. A disc, a tight buttock muscle and a narrowed spinal canal can all irritate the sciatic nerve, and a stretch that eases one can aggravate another. This guide covers seven exercises worth trying at home, which ones tend to suit which cause, and the one sign that tells you to stop.

## Do sciatica exercises actually help?

For most people, yes, though over weeks rather than in one session. Guidance from the UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence advises keeping active for back pain with or without sciatica, with structured exercise programmes for people whose pain is slower to settle. The NHS says sciatica usually gets better in a few weeks to a few months.

What exercise does is keep the spine and hips moving while irritated tissue settles, and build back the strength that pain tends to switch off. It will not make the pain vanish overnight, and a stretch that feels good for ten minutes is not proof it is the right one for you. Further down, there is a simple way to tell the difference.

## Sciatica red flags to check before you start any exercise

Check these first. If any of them apply, see a doctor or go to the emergency department today, not after trying exercises:

- loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the groin, buttocks or inner thighs
- sciatica down both legs at the same time
- weakness that is getting worse, or a foot that drags or will not lift properly
- severe pain that no position relieves, even lying down
- leg pain that started after a significant fall or accident
- fever, unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer alongside new sciatica

These are uncommon, but they are the reason every exercise below comes second to getting checked when something feels wrong.

## Which sciatica exercises suit which cause

The right sciatica exercise depends on what is irritating the nerve. The table below shows the usual patterns. It is a rough guide only, because real cases overlap and only an assessment can sort out which one is yours.

| | Disc related | Buttock muscle (piriformis) | Narrowed spinal canal (stenosis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Often feels like | Worse sitting, bending forward, coughing or sneezing | Deep buttock ache, worse sitting on hard chairs or driving | Worse standing or walking, eases when sitting or leaning forward |
| Tends to be tolerated better | Walking, prone press-ups | Figure-4 stretch, glute bridge | Knee to chest, short walks with sitting breaks |
| Go easy on | Knee to chest, long sitting, toe touches | Long sitting without breaks | Prone press-ups, standing back bends |
| More reading | [Slipped disc](/conditions/slipped-disc) | [Hip pain](/conditions/hip-pain) | [Lower back pain](/conditions/back-pain) |

Notice that prone press-ups and knee to chest pull in opposite directions. That is exactly why a generic list of "the best sciatica stretches" can help one person and annoy the next.

## 7 sciatica exercises and stretches to try at home

Start with two or three of these, not all seven. Go slowly, breathe normally, and aim for a mild stretch, never sharp pain. Twice a day is plenty to begin with.

### Walking little and often for sciatica

Walk for five to ten minutes on flat ground, a few times a day, rather than one long walk. Stop before the pain starts climbing, not after. If you have a narrowed spinal canal, plan sitting breaks along the way. A lap of the mall with a bench stop counts.

### Prone press-up for disc related sciatica

<PostFigure src="/img/sciatica-ex-prone-press-up.webp" alt="Illustration of a woman lying face down on a mat, pressing up on straight arms with her hips staying down, with an arrow showing the chest lifting" width={640} height={640} />

1. Lie on your stomach with your hands flat under your shoulders.
2. Keeping your hips on the floor, slowly straighten your arms to lift your chest.
3. Go only as far as is comfortable, hold for one or two seconds, then lower.
4. Repeat ten times.

If straight arms are too much, start by resting on your elbows for a minute. Stop if it sends pain further down your leg.

### Knee to chest stretch for sciatica

<PostFigure src="/img/sciatica-ex-knee-to-chest.webp" alt="Illustration of a woman lying on her back with one leg straight and the other knee drawn towards her chest, with an arrow showing the direction" width={640} height={640} />

1. Lie on your back, with the other leg straight or bent, whichever is more comfortable.
2. Bring one knee towards your chest and hold it with both hands, behind the thigh or just below the knee.
3. Hold for 20 to 30 seconds, then lower and switch sides.
4. Repeat three times each side.

This often feels good with a narrowed spinal canal. With disc related sciatica it can do the opposite, so watch your leg symptoms closely.

### Figure-4 stretch for the piriformis muscle

<PostFigure src="/img/sciatica-ex-figure-4.webp" alt="Woman lying on her back with one ankle crossed over the opposite knee in a figure-4 stretch for the buttock" width={500} height={500} />

1. Lie on your back with both knees bent.
2. Cross your right ankle over your left knee.
3. Hold your left thigh and gently draw it towards you until you feel a stretch deep in the right buttock.
4. Hold for 20 to 30 seconds, then switch sides. Repeat three times each side.

### Seated sciatic nerve glide

A nerve glide gently slides the irritated nerve back and forth rather than stretching it hard.

<PostFigure src="/img/sciatica-ex-nerve-glide.webp" alt="Illustration of a woman sitting on the edge of a stool, straightening one knee while tipping her head back, with arrows showing both movements" width={640} height={640} />

1. Sit tall on a firm chair or the edge of a bench.
2. Slowly straighten your sore leg while tipping your head back to look up.
3. Then bend the knee again while bringing your chin down.
4. Repeat ten times, smoothly and without holding.

You should feel very little. If tingling builds or lingers, the movement is too big, so make it smaller or leave it out.

### Glute bridge for sciatica

<PostFigure src="/img/sciatica-ex-glute-bridge.webp" alt="Illustration of a woman lying on her back with knees bent and hips lifted in a glute bridge, with an arrow pointing up under her hips" width={640} height={640} />

1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat, hip width apart.
2. Squeeze your buttocks and lift your hips until your body is in a line from shoulders to knees.
3. Hold for three to five seconds, then lower slowly.
4. Repeat eight to ten times.

### Bird dog for sciatica

<PostFigure src="/img/sciatica-ex-bird-dog.webp" alt="Illustration of a woman on hands and knees reaching one arm forward and the opposite leg back, with arrows pointing outwards" width={640} height={640} />

1. Start on your hands and knees with a flat back.
2. Reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back, keeping your hips level.
3. Hold for three to five seconds, then return and switch sides.
4. Repeat eight times each side.

### Sciatica stretches you can do at your desk

<PostFigure src="/img/sciatica-ex-desk.webp" alt="Illustration of a woman at an office desk with one ankle resting on the opposite knee, leaning forward from the hips in a seated figure-4 stretch" width={640} height={640} />

Long sitting is hard on most sciatica, and KL office hours are long. Every 30 to 45 minutes, stand up and walk to the pantry and back. In between, try a seated figure-4: sit tall, rest one ankle on the opposite knee, and lean forward slightly from the hips for 20 seconds each side. Standing back bends with your hands on your hips can feel good with disc related sciatica, but skip them if standing is what sets your leg off.

## How to tell if a sciatica exercise is helping or making it worse

Watch where the leg pain goes, not just how strong it is. If an exercise pushes symptoms further down your leg, or brings on more numbness or pins and needles, stop that exercise. The same goes for pain that is still worse two hours later or the next morning.

The opposite is often a good sign. When leg pain retreats up towards the buttock or lower back, even if your back aches a little more for a while, physiotherapists call it centralisation. Research links it with a better outlook for back pain, though it happens in fewer than half of people. Not getting it does not mean you are doing anything wrong.

A simple rule: an exercise that leaves the leg the same or better within a few hours can stay. One that leaves it worse should go, whatever any list says about it.

## When sciatica exercises are not enough

If your leg symptoms have not improved after a few weeks of sensible home exercise, or they keep coming back, it is worth finding out what is actually irritating the nerve. ## Sciatica exercises: the bottom line

Keep moving, start with two or three gentle exercises, and let your leg tell you what to keep. Pain that spreads further down the leg means stop. Pain that retreats towards the back is often a good sign. And if things are not settling after a few weeks, or any of the red flags apply, get it checked rather than stretching harder.
