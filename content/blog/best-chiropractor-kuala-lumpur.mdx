## Why "best" is hard to answer honestly

The honest answer is that no single clinic is best for every patient, because what matters most, whether that is a specific technique, a particular condition, evening opening hours, or simply proximity to your office, differs from person to person. What we can say plainly is that a good chiropractor is one you can actually verify, not just one with the most reviews or the highest place on a list.

Reviews tell you whether people liked their visit. They do not tell you whether the practitioner is registered, whether an assessment happened before anything was adjusted, or whether the approach used suited your case. Those things are checkable in a few minutes, and the rest of this covers how.

## Check registration first: ACM and MOH T&CM

Ask whether your chiropractor is registered with the Ministry of Health under the Traditional and Complementary Medicine Division, and separately whether they hold membership with the Association of Chiropractic Malaysia. Both are plain yes or no questions, and a practitioner who has genuinely trained and registered should answer either one without hesitation.

Chiropractic in Malaysia is a regulated healthcare practice under the Ministry of Health's Traditional and Complementary Medicine framework, and practitioners are expected to hold recognised qualifications and register accordingly. The Association of Chiropractic Malaysia, the profession's own body, was established in 2013, which is separate from that government registration and worth checking on its own rather than assuming the two always overlap.

Here is what makes this worth stating plainly. A 2023 survey of adults in Malaysia's Klang Valley found that only 4.8 percent were aware of the Act that actually governs chiropractic here, even though almost half of the people surveyed held a university degree. Awareness, in other words, has little to do with education level. It is simply not something most people think to ask, and a clinic is not required to volunteer it. Asking directly, and asking to see the registration number itself rather than taking a claim on trust, is the single most useful five minutes you can spend before booking anywhere in Kuala Lumpur.

## What technique they use, and why the name matters less than the process

Ask what technique your chiropractor uses and how it decides where to adjust, rather than simply which name it goes by. KL clinics use a handful of named systems: Diversified, the broadest and most commonly taught approach, instrument based methods such as the Activator, and Gonstead, the assessment led system our own chiropractors use, among others. None of them has been shown to outperform the others across the board. Reviews comparing thrust based adjustment to gentler mobilisation have generally found no consistent difference in outcomes. What differs meaningfully between the named systems is the process a chiropractor follows before adjusting, not the label on the door.

A chiropractor who can explain, in plain terms, how they decided where to work on you is telling you something useful. One who cannot, or who adjusts every patient the same way regardless of what a history or examination found, is not really practising a technique so much as a routine. We have written in more detail about how [our own six-step Gonstead assessment](/blog/gonstead-technique) works, if you want to see what a fully worked example looks like.

## What a genuinely thorough first visit looks like

A thorough first visit starts with your history and what actually brought you in, moves into a physical examination of the area and how you move, and only then, where appropriate, into an adjustment or another form of hands on care. If imaging such as an X-ray is used, it should be because the assessment pointed to something specific, not as a routine step applied to every patient. You should leave understanding what was found and what the plan is, in language you actually followed, not simply told to come back next week.

This is also where it is fair to expect honesty about limits. Chiropractic does not treat infection, fracture or disease affecting an organ, and a practitioner who takes that seriously will refer you elsewhere rather than adjust you anyway. ## Red flags worth walking away from

A few patterns are worth walking away from, in Kuala Lumpur or anywhere else: being sold a course of ten or twenty sessions before anyone has assessed you, an adjustment that happens within the first two minutes regardless of what you came in for, and a chiropractor who cannot or will not explain what they found and why they are recommending what they are recommending.

| Red flag | What it should look like instead |
|---|---|
| A course of sessions sold before you are assessed | A plan discussed only after your first assessment, reviewed honestly as it goes |
| An adjustment within minutes, before any history is taken | History taken, then an examination, then a plan you are talked through |
| Vague or evasive answers about qualifications | A straight answer, with a registration number you can ask to see |
| The same adjustment given to every patient regardless of complaint | An approach that changes based on what the assessment actually finds |

## Location, hours and cost still matter

None of this means logistics do not matter. If a clinic is inconvenient to reach or open at hours that do not fit your schedule, you are less likely to follow through on a plan even a well qualified chiropractor gives you, and consistency tends to matter more than any single visit.

Cost is worth asking about directly too. Most clinics, ours included, price a first visit as an assessment rather than a fixed package, because there is no honest way to know how many sessions someone needs before they have been seen. Ask what the first visit costs specifically and what it includes, rather than assuming from a website. A clinic that quotes an ongoing course of care before assessing you at all belongs back in the red flags above.

Where a clinic sits matters too. Ours is in Cheras, beside Sunway Velocity, reachable by the Ampang, Sri Petaling and Kajang lines if you do not drive. If most of your day is spent elsewhere in the Klang Valley, factor the commute in honestly rather than picking whichever result loads first.
