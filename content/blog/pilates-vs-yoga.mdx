Pilates and yoga both happen on a mat and both promise a stronger, looser body, so it is easy to think of them as two names for the same class. They are not. Pilates builds control of your core through slow, repeated movements. Yoga holds and flows through poses, with more stretch and more attention on the breath. If your back is the reason you are asking, the research does not crown either one, so the better pick is the one you will actually keep going to. We run yoga classes at our clinic in Cheras, not Pilates, and this guide tries to be fair to both.

## What is the difference between Pilates and yoga?

The main difference is how a movement is done. In Pilates you repeat a small, controlled movement several times while holding your trunk steady. In yoga you move into a pose and stay there for a few breaths, or flow from one pose into the next.

The rest of the differences follow from that.

| | Pilates | Yoga |
|---|---|---|
| Where it comes from | Developed by Joseph Pilates, a German trainer, in the early 20th century | An ancient practice from India; most gym and studio classes today are modern styles built on its poses |
| What a class works on | Core control, a steady spine and precise movement | Flexibility, strength and balance, often with time to slow down and relax |
| How a movement works | Five to ten slow repetitions, then on to the next exercise | Holding a pose for several breaths, or moving through a sequence |
| The breath | Timed to each repetition to help brace the core | Slow and steady, often a focus of the class in itself |
| Equipment | A mat, or a reformer, which is a sliding machine with springs and straps | A mat, with blocks, straps or a chair for support |
| How it tends to feel | A deep burn in the stomach and hips from small movements | A stretch that builds as you hold, with a pace that depends on the style |

There is plenty of overlap too. A strength focused yoga class will work your core hard, and a good Pilates class will leave you more flexible. Both are low impact, both can be scaled for beginners, and both ask you to pay attention to how you move rather than how much you lift.

## Pilates vs yoga for back pain: what the research shows

For long standing low back pain, both Pilates and yoga do better than doing nothing, and neither has been shown to beat other kinds of exercise. That is the honest summary of the two best reviews on the question, and it is more useful than it sounds.

A Cochrane review of Pilates for low back pain pooled ten trials. Compared with little or no exercise, Pilates reduced pain by a small to medium amount in the following weeks and months, but the evidence was rated low to moderate quality. When Pilates was compared with other exercise, there was no clear difference in how much people's back pain limited their day.

A Cochrane review of yoga for chronic low back pain found much the same. Yoga improved pain and function a little compared with no exercise, small enough that the authors judged it clinically unimportant, and there was probably little or no difference between yoga and other back exercise.

What this means for you:

- Either one is a reasonable choice for a back that aches from too little movement.
- Neither is a fix on its own. Think of them as ways to keep moving and get stronger.
- The exercise you keep doing every week matters more than which one you pick. If you dread a class, it will not help your back from the sofa.

## Pilates or yoga: which suits you, by situation

The right answer depends less on which is better and more on what you want and what your body can do right now. This table is a starting point, not a rule.

| Your situation | Leans towards | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Stiff back and neck after a week at a desk | Either | Both get you moving after long hours in one position. Yoga gives more stretch, Pilates more strength through the trunk |
| You want a stronger core and better posture | Pilates, or a strength focused yoga class | Pilates is built around core control; some yoga classes are too |
| You are not very flexible | Pilates, or a beginner yoga class | Pilates asks for less range of movement at the start. Many yoga poses can be adapted with a block or a chair |
| You are older, or getting down to the floor is hard | Chair yoga, or chair or reformer Pilates | Both keep you off the floor and give you something steady to hold |
| You are pregnant | A class made for pregnancy, in either | Check with your doctor first, and tell the instructor how far along you are so lying and twisting positions can be adapted |
| You are coming back from an injury | Pilates with a well trained instructor, after an assessment | Pilates is widely used in rehab, but the plan should start from what was injured |
| Stress, tight shoulders and poor sleep | Yoga | Slow breathing and time to relax are part of most classes |

Many people do both, Pilates for strength on one day and yoga for stretch on another, and that is a sensible mix. If posture is the real goal, our [posture correction](/services/posture-correction) programme looks at how you actually sit and work, which a class cannot see.

## Mat Pilates, reformer Pilates and yoga styles for beginners in KL

Both Pilates and yoga come in very different versions, so the class name tells you more than the word Pilates or yoga on its own.

**Pilates comes in two main forms.** Mat Pilates uses your own body weight, sometimes with a small ball, ring or band. It is the cheaper way in and you can carry it home. Reformer Pilates uses a sliding machine with springs that can add resistance or help you through a movement, which makes it easier to adjust for beginners and harder for the experienced. Reformer classes usually cost more because of the equipment and smaller groups. You may also see clinical Pilates, which is usually run by a physiotherapist and aimed at people coming back from injury.

**Yoga comes in many styles, and the pace varies a lot.**

- **Hatha** is usually slower, with poses held and explained. A good first class.
- **Vinyasa** or flow links poses together with the breath and can feel like a workout.
- **Yin** holds poses for minutes at a time and is mostly about stretch and stillness.
- **Hot yoga** is done in a heated room. If you are new, have a heart or blood pressure condition, or are pregnant, start somewhere cooler.
- **Chair yoga** is done seated or beside a chair, for anyone who prefers not to get down to the floor.

Whichever you choose, tell the instructor you are new and mention any back, neck or joint problem before the class starts. A good instructor will offer a different version of a move rather than let you push through it.

## When to get your back checked before starting Pilates or yoga

Most people with an ordinary stiff back can start either class straight away. Get your back assessed first if any of these apply:

- pain, numbness or tingling that spreads into a leg or an arm
- back pain that is new, getting worse, or wakes you at night
- pain after a fall, a knock or a road accident
- weakness in a leg, or changes in bladder or bowel control, which needs urgent medical care
- osteoporosis or a past spinal fracture, since deep forward bends and loaded twists may not be suitable
- a recent slipped disc, until the worst of the pain has settled

## Pilates vs yoga: the bottom line

Pilates trains control of your core through small, repeated movements. Yoga holds and flows through poses with more stretch and more breath.
